# Marking calibration

## Scale
If the spec or rubric gives its own scale or band boundaries, use those. Otherwise use the standard Australian university bands:

| Band | Mark | What the work looks like |
|---|---|---|
| HD (High Distinction) | 85–100 | Exceptional. Every criterion met at the highest level. Original, critical insight; synthesis rather than summary; virtually error-free. Code is correct including edge cases, well designed and tested. Maths is fully correct, rigorous and clearly justified. Rare: roughly the top 5–10% of a cohort. |
| D (Distinction) | 75–84 | Strong. All requirements met, clear critical analysis, well supported. Only minor flaws. |
| CR (Credit) | 65–74 | Competent. Requirements met and generally sound, but more descriptive than analytical, with some gaps or errors that do not undermine the whole. Most solid work lands here. |
| P (Pass) | 50–64 | Meets the minimum. Significant weaknesses: largely descriptive, notable errors, or requirements only partly met. |
| F (Fail) | 0–49 | Key requirements not met, major errors, incomplete, or off-task. |

The typical median mark in a university course is about 65–70. Your marks should reflect that distribution.

## Rules
1. **Score criteria first, then total.** The overall mark follows from the criteria, not from your overall impression.
2. **Do not round up across a band boundary.** If the work sits between two bands, give the lower one unless you can name specific evidence for the higher.
3. **A mark of 85 or more** requires you to be able to name specific evidence of excellence for every major criterion. If you cannot, it is not an HD.
4. **A missing required component scores zero for that criterion.**
5. **Effort, length and good intentions earn nothing.** Fluent prose does not rescue weak analysis. A correct final answer with no working earns little. Code that does not run, or fails a core requirement, cannot score well on correctness.
6. **Match the level.** Judge against what the spec asks at its year level (first-year vs final-year or postgraduate; infer from the unit code or wording). Do not grade leniently "for a student".
7. **Word count.** Apply the spec's penalty if it states one. If not, many courses allow ±10%; beyond that, deduct under presentation/compliance and say so.
8. **No anchoring.** Do not guess what mark the student wants or expects.

## Inflation traps to avoid
- Rewarding an interesting topic instead of how well it is handled.
- Rewarding confident tone or polished formatting as if it were evidence.
- Assuming a section is fine because it is long.
- Assuming code works because it looks tidy. Check the execution evidence.
- Giving full marks for a criterion just because nothing is obviously wrong. Full marks need positive evidence of excellence.
